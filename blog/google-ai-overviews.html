What Google actually says about AI Overviews, and what it doesn't.

Want to rank in Google AI Overviews? Here's what Google's own documentation says, the myths it rules out, and what that means for your site.

So much advice, so little of it from Google

If you've searched for how to rank in AI Overviews, you've probably found a hundred different tricks. Add this file. Use that schema. Rewrite everything in short chunks. Most of it comes from people guessing.

I work in SEO and content every day, and I'd rather work from the source. Google has published two official guides on its AI features: AI features and your website and a longer one, Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search, last updated in July 2026.

I read both closely. Here's what Google actually says, what it rules out, and the questions it leaves open.

What Google officially says about AI Overviews SEO

Google's message is simple: AI Overviews run on regular Search, so regular SEO is how you get in. These are the points it states outright.

AI Overviews use the core ranking systems. Google says its AI features "are rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems." The AI retrieves pages from the Search index and builds its answer from them, with links back.

There are no extra technical requirements. To show as a supporting link, "a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet." Google adds: "There are no additional technical requirements."

One question becomes many searches. Google uses "query fan-out," running several related searches to build a fuller answer. Its example: "how to fix a lawn that's full of weeds" might also search "best herbicides for lawns" and "how to prevent weeds in lawn."

Unique content matters most. Google says content people find "unique, compelling, and useful" will likely influence your presence in AI search "more than any of the other suggestions in this guide." It contrasts commodity content like "7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers" with first-hand pieces that go beyond common knowledge.

Local details count. Google points to Business Profiles and Merchant Center as ways for businesses and products to show in AI responses.

You can measure it. Search Console has a Generative AI performance report. Google also says clicks from results pages with AI Overviews "are higher quality," meaning people tend to spend more time on the site.

AI Overviews often don't show. Google says they appear only when its systems decide they add something to classic Search, "and as such, often don't trigger."

The myths Google rules out

Google's newer guide has a whole section called "what you don't need to do." It says many AEO and GEO "hacks" "aren't effective or supported by how Google Search actually works." For Google Search, you can ignore:

  • llms.txt and other AI files. "Google Search itself doesn't use them."
  • Chunking your content. "There's no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI to better understand it." There's also "no ideal page length."
  • Rewriting just for AI. Google says its systems understand synonyms, so you don't need every long-tail variation of a keyword.
  • Chasing fake mentions. Seeking "inauthentic mentions" across the web "isn't as helpful as it might seem."
  • Overdoing structured data. It "isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add." Keep using it for rich results.
  • Pages for every query variation. Making them mainly to manipulate AI answers breaks the scaled content abuse policy.

What Google doesn't say

The guides are clear on what not to do. They're much quieter on a few things people really want to know.

  • How sources get picked. Google says AI Overviews draw on its ranking systems, but it doesn't say whether ranking first means being cited, or how many pages it considers.
  • What happens to your traffic. Google says AI Overview clicks are higher quality. It doesn't publish how many clicks sites get compared with a classic result.
  • Anything about other AI tools. These guides cover Google only. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot have their own crawlers and their own ways of choosing sources.

One small flag: Google's older page says AI feature traffic is counted inside the regular Performance report. The newer guide points to a separate Generative AI performance report. I'd check both in Search Console.

What this means for your small business site

You don't need a special AI Overviews strategy. You need solid SEO and content that only you could write. Here's where I'd put your time.

  1. Check you're eligible. Make sure your key pages are indexed and not blocked by robots.txt, your hosting or a stray noindex tag. Search Console's URL Inspection tool shows you what Google sees.
  2. Update your Google Business Profile. If you serve a local area, this is the quickest win.
  3. Write from experience. Swap generic tips for your real projects, prices, process and local knowledge. That's the content Google says stands out.
  4. Organise pages for people. Clear headings, short paragraphs, a direct answer near the top. It reads better, and it's easier to quote.
  5. Watch the right numbers. Look at the Generative AI report in Search Console, and track enquiries and calls, not just rankings.

If someone tries to sell you an llms.txt file or an "AI schema package" as your AI Overviews fix, you now know what Google says about that.

FAQ

Can I opt out of Google AI Overviews?+

Google says AI is built into Search, so the controls are the usual ones. Use nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet or noindex to limit what shows from your pages. Blocking Googlebot would also remove you from regular Search.

Do I need schema to appear in AI Overviews?+

No. Google says structured data isn't required for its AI features. It still helps with rich results, so it's worth keeping, and it should always match what's visible on the page.

Is AEO or GEO different from SEO for Google?+

For Google, mostly no. Its guide says the foundations are the same SEO best practices. Other AI tools may behave differently, which I cover in how to rank in AI search.
